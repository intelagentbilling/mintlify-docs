Registration doesn't submit anything. Once you have a billing number and MC EDT
access you still need a way to turn an encounter into a ministry-format claim
file — and a routine that keeps you from losing money to the things nobody
teaches in residency.

## How claims reach the ministry

There are three ways, and the middle one is where most physicians land.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Direct MC EDT" icon="file-code">
    You build ministry-format claim files yourself and upload them through your
    own GO Secure account.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Self-billing platform" icon="laptop-medical">
    You enter the encounter and codes. The platform handles file format, timing,
    error reconciliation and remittance matching.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Full-service billing" icon="user-tie">
    A service does data entry, coding review, submission and follow-up from your
    shift lists or chart access.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Direct MC EDT** means writing or buying software that produces the exact file
format, and maintaining it as the specification changes. Not worth it unless you
have technical resources you're already paying for.

**Self-billing** keeps you in control and oversight, without the file format ever
being your problem. This is IntelAGENT's Standard plan.

**Full-service** is the highest convenience and the highest cost — you hand over
encounter information and someone else does the rest. That's IntelAGENT's Premium
plan, where a dedicated billing agent reviews your claims, suggests optimizations
and manages rejections.

Fees are charged on what you're actually **paid**, not what you claim, and there
are no setup fees. On Standard the 0.25% is **capped at $39.99**, so a heavy
billing month costs the same as an average one — $10,000 paid is $25, and
$50,000 paid is still $39.99.

New accounts get a **3-month free trial** — full Standard features, no charge
until it ends. Full detail on [Plans & Pricing](/docs/billing/plans-pricing).

## Two habits worth building now

These two save more money in a first year than any coding trick.

### Bill the same day

Enter claims at the end of the day, while the details are still fresh. Batching a
week's worth costs you in three ways at once: transcription errors, codes you
forget you earned, and premiums that only exist if you remember the time of day
you saw the patient.

Five minutes at the end of a shift is worth hundreds of dollars over a year — and
it keeps you clear of the [18th-of-the-month
cutoff](/docs/new-physician/edt-access#know-the-18th).

<Card title="Bill a whole roster in one pass" icon="users" href="/docs/guides/roster-billing">
  If you round on the same patients, a roster turns the daily habit into seconds
  rather than minutes.
</Card>

### Reconcile every remittance advice

The ministry releases a remittance advice around the **20th of each month**. Read
it.

Rejected claims can usually be corrected and resubmitted, and that's the smaller
half of the value. The larger half is that systematic rejections — a wrong
specialty code, a stale health card version, a diagnosis that doesn't go with the
code — **recur every month until you fix the cause**. One review catches a pattern
that would otherwise bill against you all year.

<Card title="Fix a rejected claim" icon="magnifying-glass" href="/docs/guides/find-and-fix-claims">
  Find what the ministry refused or paid short, correct it, and send it back.
</Card>

<Tip>
IntelAGENT matches remittance advices against your submitted claims automatically,
so reconciling is reading a list of exceptions rather than comparing two
documents. See [Remittance Advices](/docs/web-app/reports/remittance-advices).
</Tip>
